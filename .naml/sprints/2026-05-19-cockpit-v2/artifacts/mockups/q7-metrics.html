Question 07 / Metrics

What numbers earn screen space — and where?

Four surfaces carry metrics: the always-visible right rail, the dashboard hero, the per-sprint aux strip, and the per-slice stats row. Each option below skews what those surfaces emphasize: a balanced cockpit, a cost-conscious skew, or a quality-conscious skew.
← all ← Q6b
RECOMMENDATION
Option A — Balanced cockpit. Right rail mirrors CodexBar (Session %, Weekly %, Today cost + tokens). Hero carries the four most decision-relevant project rollups: Today $, Tier-1 hit %, Avg sprint duration, Sprints this week. Sprint aux strip shows live elapsed, ETA, cost, lanes, slices, last-tick. Slice stats row carries the actionable in-flight signals: cost, tokens, retry, last gate, session-id with action buttons. Tier-1 hit rate gets prime real estate because it's the single best health indicator for the merge pipeline — when it drops, the whole sprint slows down.

Pipeline health is the conversation. Tier-1 hit rate, LGTM rate, slice fail rate, review iterations rule the chrome. Cost recedes to a single line in the slice stats. Best when you're tuning the system itself (skills, ADRs, gate config) rather than shipping features.

  • Tier-1 trend is front-and-center
  • Gate failures named (not just counted)
COSTS
  • Easy to blow weekly quota un-noticed
  • Numbers are mostly post-event ratios (not live)
  • Stress signal during a failure cascade
Pick a metrics skew. Reply A, B, or C. Or a hybrid, e.g. "A for the rail, B for the hero".
Decisions inside whichever you pick:
  • Tier-1 hit-rate window — last 7 days, last 30 sprints, or all-time? My pick: 7 days (responsive to recent regressions).
  • Cost-projection method — linear extrapolation from current burn (simple), or "remaining slices × p50 per-slice cost" (smarter)? Default to linear until history.
  • Per-sprint summary metrics after merge — which to log? I'd capture: total cost, total duration, tier-1 hit, retry count, LGTM rate, mean ctx peak, TTFP. So we can graph trends in Settings > Health.
  • Anything I'm missing? One signal I'm not tracking yet: review/implementer disagreement rate — when the reviewer asks for changes and the implementer's fix gets a fresh LGTM. If that disagreement is high, you have prompt drift between the two roles. Worth surfacing?
