Question 06 / Lane layout

How are the lanes spatially arranged?

With parallel_lanes = 3 by default, every running slice is assigned to one of three workers (each with its own git worktree + Claude session). The question: does the screen organize work by lane, by time, or by slice?
← all ← Q5
RECOMMENDATION
Option A — vertical kanban columns, one per lane. Three side-by-side columns, each carrying its lane's currently-active slice card + recently-completed slice underneath + a "waiting for next" empty slot when idle. Lane utilization is visible at a glance — if a column is idle while the queue strip below shows pending slices, you instantly see "we have unused capacity, why?". Matches the V2 design-doc's "vertical lane layout" directive 1:1.

Lane columns mean lane utilization is the visible question, not just slice progress. If two columns are busy and one is idle while ready slices are queued — you see the bottleneck (probably DAG width, not lanes). The queue strip underneath shows what's competing for the next free lane.

WINS
  • Lane utilization is the headline
  • Idle lane = visible empty slot
  • Queue strip shows competition for capacity
  • Maps 1:1 to V2 doc directive
COSTS
  • ~320px per column — feels tight at 3 lanes
  • Tough to scale visually past 6 lanes
  • Time ordering not implicit (need timestamps)

Time on x-axis, lanes as horizontal swimlanes. Block widths show slice duration; "now" line is the cyan vertical bar. Best for understanding when things happened and how long each took. Looks like a build pipeline / Gantt chart.

WINS
  • Time ordering instantly readable
  • Slice duration is visual (block width)
  • Lane idle gaps show "starved" moments
COSTS
  • Slice detail squeezed inside narrow blocks
  • Need a zoom/pan control for long sprints
  • Drowns out per-slice telemetry
OPTION C

Flat list · slices first, lane as a colored chip

Slice-first. Lane assignment is a chip on the left, not a spatial axis. Most compact — fits 12+ rows on screen — but lanes lose their spatial identity. Best when you have 5+ lanes (columns would get too narrow).

WINS
  • Most slices visible at once
  • Scales gracefully to 6+ lanes
  • Sortable by state / duration / cost
COSTS
  • Lane utilization hidden (can't see idle lanes)
  • Less spatial / cockpit-y
  • Doesn't match the V2 doc directive

Side decisions on A:
  • What does an idle lane show? My pick: most-recent completed slice on top + dashed "READY · NEXT SLICE WILL LAND HERE" empty slot. Or fully blank with just the "READY" placeholder?
  • Column count past 3 lanes — if user bumps parallel_lanes = 6, do we (a) shrink columns, (b) horizontal-scroll, or (c) auto-switch to Option C's flat list? My pick: shrink to 4, scroll past 4.
  • Queue strip placement — below the lanes (visible always) or behind a "show queue" toggle (cleaner)? I'd keep it visible — it's the answer to "why is a lane idle?".
