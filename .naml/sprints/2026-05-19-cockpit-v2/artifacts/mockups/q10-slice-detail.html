OPEN-IN-TERMINAL · GATING LOGICwhy this matters

Claude sessions are single-owner. If naml's lane worker is mid-conversation with a session and you run claude --resume <id> in a terminal, the conversation gets pulled into your terminal and the lane's process loses its handle on it — the run is effectively interrupted and the slice can end in a corrupt state.

So the button is gated by slice state:

  • Locked in setup · work · pr — naml is actively driving the session.
  • Unlocked in held · review · merged · failed · needs_human_review · abandoned · blocked_upstream — implementer session is at rest.

HOLD & UNLOCK TERMINAL is the safe intervention path mid-run. It (1) signals the lane to stop starting new turns, (2) waits for the in-flight turn to settle, (3) flips state to held, (4) unlocks the terminal button. RESUME from held re-engages naml.

Side decisions on A:
  • Drawer width — 60% as shown, or 50% (more cockpit visible) / 70% (more drawer canvas)?
  • "Open in terminal" semantics — opens a new Terminal.app window running claude --resume <session-id> at the worktree path. Confirm that's the right shape; same flow that status command uses in the prototype.
  • Intervention actions — HOLD (pause this slice), MARK FAILED, SKIP. Want a fourth? Common candidates: "force re-run from setup", "promote to HITL", "merge from here".
  • Keyboard nav — j/k to step through slices while drawer is open, esc to close, cmd-shift-c to copy session id. Worth it, or keep keyboard minimal?
  • Side-by-side compare — two slices open at once is rare but powerful (compare last successful vs current failing). Defer to v2, or design for it now?
